02 / STATISTICAL METHODS

Three ways to ask “how sure?”

METHOD 01

Base rate

The fraction of summer days with a valid measured daily maximum above 35°C, separately for each period.

p = hot_days / valid_summer_daysShow the numerator and denominator, not just the percentage.
METHOD 02

Year-block bootstrap

Resample entire summers, with replacement, 10,000 times within each period to estimate how the rate and temperature shifts vary.

95% range = [q2.5, q97.5]We resample years because successive days within a summer are not independent.
METHOD 03

Permutation reference

Shuffle the 60 summer-year period labels 10,000 times, recomputing the hot-day rate difference each time.

observed difference vs shuffled nullShows a no-period-difference reference, not a weather prediction.
Interpretation rule

Question two compares the pooled mean summer daily maximum and its 95th percentile (the hot tail). Both changes get year-block bootstrap intervals; if an interval spans zero, we do not claim that change is detected.

04 / REPRODUCIBILITY

One test matters most.

After the collector commits verified raw files, another teammate disconnects from the network, restarts the notebook kernel and runs all cells from scratch. The same result must be reproducible from the cached inputs.

File hashes, fixed seeds, missingness counts and matching plotted numbers are checked before any final verdict is published.

The export command is only run after the notebook verifies actual cached observations.

WHAT WE CANNOT CLAIM

ERA5 describes a gridded reconstruction, not a city thermometer. Rare events can produce wide ranges; whole-year resampling does not remove all long-term dependence; the study describes period differences rather than attributing causes or forecasting future temperatures.
